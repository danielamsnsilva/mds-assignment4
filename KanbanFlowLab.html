Kanban is a strategy for optimizing the flow of value. Work is made visible on a board, the amount of work in progress is limited, and new work is pulled only when there is capacity for it.

Three practices from The Kanban Guide (2020)

01
Define and visualize the workflow

Agree what a work item is, where work starts and finishes, the states in between, and the WIP limits.

02
Actively manage items in the workflow

Control WIP, unblock work quickly, and keep items from ageing unnecessarily.

03
Improve the workflow

Use the flow measures to find bottlenecks and change the workflow in small, measured steps.

Reading a CFD. The vertical distance across the "in progress" band is WIP. The horizontal distance is approximate cycle time. The slope of the Done band is throughput. A band that keeps widening means work is entering faster than it leaves.
Little's Law
Avg cycle time = Avg WIP ÷ Avg throughput
{{ law.ct }}days average cycle time

Holds for averages in a stable system, where items arrive about as fast as they leave. Halving WIP at the same throughput halves cycle time.

Forecasting with percentiles. Instead of an average, quote the 85th percentile: "85% of our items finish in {{ data.p85 }} days or less." Darker bars sit above that line. Customers get a probability, not a promise.

Darker bars are the slowest 15% of items. Those outliers are often worth discussing in a retrospective: what blocked them?
